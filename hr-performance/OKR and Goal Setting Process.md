# OKR and Goal Setting Process

Last Updated: May 12, 2026

## 1. Overview

Lutervyn uses the **OKR (Objectives and Key Results)** framework to align individual efforts with company-wide strategy.

## 2. Definitions

- **Objectives**: Ambitious, qualitative, and time-bound goals (The "What").
- **Key Results**: Concrete, measurable outcomes that indicate the objective has been met (The "How").

## 3. The OKR Cycle

### 3.1 Quarterly Cadence
1. **Week -2**: Leadership sets top-level company OKRs.
2. **Week -1**: Departments and teams set their OKRs in alignment with company goals.
3. **Week 1**: All OKRs are finalized and shared publicly in the [Internal Wiki](../knowledge-management/Internal%20Wiki%20and%20Knowledge%20Base%20Management.md).
4. **Mid-Quarter**: Progress check-in and adjustment if necessary.
5. **Week +1 (Next Quarter)**: Scoring and retrospective on the previous quarter's OKRs.

## 4. Setting Effective OKRs

### 4.1 Guidelines
- **Limit**: No more than 3-5 Objectives and 3-5 Key Results per objective.
- **Stretch**: OKRs should be challenging. Achieving 70-80% of a stretch OKR is considered a success.
- **Measurable**: If it doesn't have a number, it's not a Key Result.

## 5. Tools

- We use **Lattice** (or a similar tool) to track and report on OKR progress.

## 6. Relationship to Compensation

- OKR achievement is a significant input into performance reviews, but it is not the *only* factor.
- We reward high impact and effort even if a particularly ambitious stretch goal was not 100% achieved.
