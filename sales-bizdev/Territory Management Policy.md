# Territory Management Policy

Last Updated: May 12, 2026

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this policy is to define how sales territories are assigned, managed, and adjusted to ensure fair opportunity and maximum market coverage.

### 1.2 Objectives
- Optimize sales team productivity.
- Ensure equitable distribution of potential.
- Prevent internal competition and conflict.
- Enhance customer experience through consistent coverage.

## 2. Territory Definitions

### 2.1 Geographic Territories
- Defined by state, region, or country boundaries.
- Assigned based on historical performance and market potential.
- Example: Northeast US, Western Europe, APAC.

### 2.2 Segment-Based Territories
- Defined by company size, industry vertical, or revenue.
- Strategic Accounts: Global enterprises.
- Mid-Market: Medium-sized businesses.
- SMB: Small to medium businesses.

### 2.3 Named Account Lists
- Specific high-value accounts assigned to individual Account Executives (AEs).
- Usually reserved for Strategic or Enterprise segments.

## 3. Assignment Process

### 3.1 Initial Assignment
- Assigned upon hire or during annual planning.
- Based on AE experience, seniority, and location.
- Approved by the VP of Sales.

### 3.2 Territory Rebalancing
- Reviewed annually or semi-annually.
- Adjustments made based on:
  - Over/under-penetration of markets.
  - Team size changes (hiring/attrition).
  - Shifts in company strategy.
- 30-day notice provided for major changes.

## 4. Conflict Resolution (Lead Ownership)

### 4.1 Inbound Leads
- Leads are routed based on the primary territory owner.
- If a lead falls into multiple categories, Geographic ownership takes precedence unless it is a Named Account.

### 4.2 Lead Poaching
- Sales team members are prohibited from actively prospecting in territories assigned to others.
- If an AE accidentally prospects a lead in another's territory, they must hand it over immediately.

### 4.3 Split Credits
- In cases where multiple AEs contribute significantly to a deal (e.g., global headquarters in one territory, implementation in another), credit may be split.
- Split ratios are determined by the Sales Manager (typically 50/50).

## 5. Account Transitions

### 5.1 Handover Procedures
- When an AE leaves or a territory is reassigned, a formal handover must occur.
- Outgoing AE must document:
  - Current status of all active opportunities.
  - Key contacts and relationships.
  - Future follow-up dates.

### 5.2 Protection Period
- Deals in the final stages (Negotiation/Closing) may remain with the original owner for up to 30 days after a territory change, subject to manager approval.

## 6. Reporting and Auditing

### 6.1 Accuracy
- AEs are responsible for ensuring account data (Address, Industry, Revenue) is accurate in the CRM to ensure correct territory routing.

### 6.2 Compliance Audits
- Periodic audits will be conducted to ensure leads are being routed and managed according to this policy.
