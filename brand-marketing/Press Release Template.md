## Press Release Writing Guidelines

### 1. Headline Guidelines

**Best Practices**
- Action: use active verbs
- Specific: be specific and concrete
- News: lead with news
- Length: 10-15 words typical
- Benefit: include customer benefit

**Example Headlines**
- "Lutervyn Launches AI-Powered Analytics, Enabling 50% Faster Decision-Making"
- "Enterprise Customers Choose Lutervyn for Security-First Approach"
- "Lutervyn Announces $[X]M Series [X] Funding to Expand Market Leadership"

### 2. Dateline Guidelines

**Format**
[City, State] – [Month XX, 20XX]

**Timing**
- Pre-release: 6 weeks advance
- Day before: embargo
- Release: 12:00 PM ET Tuesday typical
- Follow-up: day after available

### 3. Opening Paragraph (The Lede)

**Content**
- Company: who is announcing
- What: specific announcement
- When: timing/availability
- Why: significance
- Who: affected parties

**Structure**
- Sentence 1: company announces [what]
- Sentence 2: impact or benefit
- Sentence 3: additional detail or context

**Example**
"Lutervyn Inc., a leader in [category], today announced [specific feature], enabling customers to [benefit]. The [feature] is effective [date] and available to all [audience]. This announcement addresses the growing need for [market need]."

### 4. Quote Guidelines

**Best Practices**
- Executive: from recognized executive
- Insight: share insight, not obvious
- Benefit: explain customer benefit
- Length: 2-3 sentences maximum
- Authentic: sound natural and authentic

**Quote Structure**
- Sentence 1: significance statement
- Sentence 2: customer/market benefit
- Sentence 3: forward-looking statement

**Example**
"This announcement represents a significant milestone in our mission to [mission]," said [Name], [Title] at Lutervyn. "By delivering [feature], we're enabling customers to [benefit]. We're excited to see how customers leverage this capability to [anticipated result]."

### 5. Length Guidelines

- Optimal: 400-600 words
- Minimum: 300 words
- Maximum: 800 words
- Scannable: use short paragraphs
- Readable: 8th-grade reading level

### 6. Tone Guidelines

- Professional: credible and authoritative
- Newsworthy: what's new and significant
- Clear: simple and direct language
- Benefit: focus on customer benefit
- Honest: truthful and accurate

### 7. What to Avoid

**Don'ts**
- All caps: avoid shouting
- Jargon: minimize technical terms
- Hype: avoid exaggeration
- Fluff: remove unnecessary words
- Poor grammar: proofread carefully
- Corporate speak: use plain language
- Unsourced claims: cite sources for claims

### 8. Press Release Distribution

**Channels**
- Email: direct to media list
- Wire service: PR Newswire, BusinessWire
- Website: press/news section
- Social media: LinkedIn, Twitter
- Internal: employee communication

**Timing**
- Media: 24 hours advance embargo
- Press release: simultaneous release
- Social: within same day
- Internal: same day or day after

### 9. Follow-Up Activities

**Tactics**
- Media outreach: follow-up calls to journalists
- Content: blog post elaborating
- Social: social media promotion
- Customer: customer communication
- Events: presentation at upcoming event
- Analytics: track coverage

### 10. Press Release Variations

**Variations by Type**

**Product Announcement**
- Focus: product features and benefits
- Content: demo information, pricing
- Timing: at launch date
- Tone: exciting and benefit-focused

**Funding Announcement**
- Focus: funding amount and use of funds
- Content: investor list, valuation
- Timing: announcement day
- Tone: growth and investment optimism

**Partnership Announcement**
- Focus: partnership details and benefits
- Content: partner information, integration
- Timing: effective date
- Tone: complementary and collaborative

**Leadership Change**
- Focus: new executive and background
- Content: biography, role, goals
- Timing: effective date
- Tone: professional and positive

**Milestone/Achievement**
- Focus: achievement significance
- Content: context and impact
- Timing: achievement date
- Tone: celebratory but professional
