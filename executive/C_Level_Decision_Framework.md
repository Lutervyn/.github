# C-Level Decision Framework

Last Updated: May 12, 2026

## 1. Decision Authority Levels

### 1.1 CEO Authority

**Sole CEO Authority** (up to $100K investment):
- Annual strategic goals
- Company vision and positioning
- Major personnel decisions (C-level hiring/termination)
- New market entry strategy
- Product positioning and branding
- Board and investor relations
- Budget allocations within approved plan
- Expenditures up to $100K

**Budget Thresholds**:
- Up to $10K: Verbal approval from CEO
- $10K-$50K: Written CEO approval required
- $50K-$100K: CFO + CEO written approval
- Over $100K: Board approval required

### 1.2 CFO Authority

**Finance Decisions** (CFO has authority for):
- Annual budget development and approval
- Monthly/quarterly variance management
- Payroll and benefits administration
- Banking relationships and credit lines
- Financial reporting and audit coordination
- Treasury management and cash deployment
- Accounts payable/receivable policies
- Tax planning and compliance

**Budget Approvals**:
- <$25K: CFO approval
- $25K-$100K: CFO + CEO approval
- >$100K: Board approval

### 1.3 CTO Authority

**Technology/Engineering Decisions**:
- Technology stack and architecture decisions
- Infrastructure and cloud deployments
- Security policies and standards
- Vendor selection for tech services
- Product technical roadmap
- Engineering team structure and hiring
- Development process improvements
- DevOps and deployment strategies

**Budget Approvals**:
- <$50K: CTO approval
- $50K-$100K: CTO + CEO approval
- >$100K: Board approval

### 1.4 CPO Authority

**Product Decisions**:
- Product roadmap and release planning
- Feature prioritization and design
- Customer feedback incorporation
- UX/UI standards
- Product portfolio decisions
- Launch and go-to-market strategy
- Pricing and packaging decisions
- Customer success strategy

**Budget Approvals**:
- <$25K: CPO approval
- $25K-$50K: CPO + CEO approval
- >$50K: Board approval

### 1.5 COO Authority

**Operational Decisions**:
- Operational process improvements
- Facilities and real estate
- Office management and expenses
- Vendor management (non-tech)
- Travel and expense policies
- Administrative functions
- Compliance and legal operations
- HR administration

**Budget Approvals**:
- <$25K: COO approval
- $25K-$75K: COO + CEO approval
- >$75K: Board approval

## 3. Escalation Triggers

**Situations Requiring Escalation**:

**To CFO**:
- Budget variance >10%
- Unexpected major expense
- Revenue or cash flow concerns
- Financial reporting issues
- Vendor payment disputes

**To CEO**:
- Decision affects multiple departments
- Material business impact (>$50K)
- Strategic implications
- Conflict between departments
- Sensitive personnel matter
- Customer or investor issue

**To Board**:
- Major strategic decision
- Capital raise or significant financing
- M&A or partnership
- Significant loss or liability
- Executive team changes
- Regulatory or compliance issues
- Major product pivot

**To Legal**:
- Contracts or agreements
- Regulatory compliance
- Employment matters
- Intellectual property
- Litigation or disputes
- Governance questions

## 4. Standard Decision Process

### 4.1 Five-Step Decision Framework

**Step 1: Problem Definition** (Owner: Decision requester)
- What decision needs to be made?
- Why is it needed and by when?
- What's the business impact?
- Who is affected?

**Step 2: Analysis & Options** (Owner: Decision requester)
- Identify 3+ alternatives
- Analyze pros/cons of each
- Financial impact analysis
- Risk assessment
- Timeline and resource requirements

**Step 3: Stakeholder Input** (Owner: Decision requester)
- Identify relevant stakeholders
- Get input from affected parties
- Legal and compliance review if needed
- Risk management assessment

**Step 4: Recommendation** (Owner: Decision requester)
- Present analysis to decision authority
- Clear recommendation with rationale
- Alternative options listed
- Timeline for implementation

**Step 5: Decision & Communication** (Owner: Decision maker)
- Decision maker approves, rejects, or modifies
- Decision documented in writing
- All stakeholders notified
- Implementation assigned with accountability

### 4.2 Decision Documentation

**All Decisions >$10K Must Include**:
- Decision title and date
- Business rationale
- Options considered and rejected
- Financial impact
- Risk assessment and mitigation
- Implementation timeline
- Owner and accountability
- Approval signatures

**Storage**: Decisions indexed by year, amount, and category

## 5. Conflict Resolution in Decisions

### 5.1 Interdepartmental Conflicts

**Escalation Path**:
1. Department heads meet and resolve (3 days)
2. If unresolved, CEO facilitation (1 week)
3. CEO makes decision or escalates to Board

**Examples**:
- Product roadmap conflicts between CTO and CPO
- Budget allocation disputes between departments
- Customer request conflicts with tech constraints

### 5.2 Decision Delays or Bottlenecks

**If Decision Not Made Within SLA**:
- Escalate to next authority level
- CEO or decision authority reviews issue
- Timeline accelerated
- Decision made by escalation authority

**Decision SLAs**:
- <$10K: 3 business days
- $10K-$50K: 5 business days
- $50K-$100K: 10 business days
- >$100K: Board cycle (typically 30 days)

## 6. Emergency Decision Procedures

**Used When**:
- Immediate decision needed (same-day)
- Security or critical issue
- Time-sensitive business opportunity
- Customer crisis

**Process**:
1. Decision requester notifies CEO
2. CEO assembles decision makers (if time allows)
3. Decision made with available input
4. Decision documented immediately
5. Full analysis and documentation within 3 days
6. Board notified at next meeting if >$50K

**Emergency Authority**:
- CEO has unilateral authority in emergencies
- Authority reports to Board within 48 hours
- Board reviews and ratifies or overrides decision

## 7. Board-Level Decisions

### 7.1 Board-Only Decisions

- Fundraising and capital raises
- Merger, acquisition, or sale of company
- Liquidation or dissolution
- Bylaws and charter amendments
- Executive compensation >certain threshold
- Related party transactions
- Board composition and elections
- Annual budget >threshold
- Debt or major financing

### 7.2 Board Decision Process

1. CEO/management presents issue to board
2. Board discussion and questions (30 min)
3. Board motion and vote
4. Minutes document decision
5. CEO executes decision
6. Investor notification if applicable

## 8. Reversibility and Re-decisions

### 8.1 Decision Reversibility

**Easily Reversible Decisions**:
- Can be changed without major consequence
- Example: Office policy on work from home
- Authority: Same level or higher can modify

**Harder to Reverse**:
- Some financial impact to reverse
- Example: Vendor selection for 2-year contract
- Authority: Authority that made decision + higher if needed

**Nearly Irreversible**:
- Significant cost or consequence to reverse
- Example: Product discontinuation, major layoff
- Authority: Requires board approval to reverse

### 8.2 Re-decision Criteria

A decision can be revisited if:
- New material information emerges
- Business circumstances significantly change
- Performance metrics are off track
- 12+ months have passed (re-evaluation)
- Stakeholder consensus shifts

## 9. Decision Learning and Feedback

### 9.1 Post-Decision Review

**For All Major Decisions** (>$50K or strategic):
- Scheduled review: 30, 90, and 180 days post-decision
- Actual vs. projected results
- Learnings and adjustments
- Success metrics met?
- What would we do differently?

### 9.2 Decision Effectiveness Metrics

- Decision made on time (met SLA)
- Stakeholders accept/support decision
- Desired business outcomes achieved
- No unintended negative consequences
- Decision efficiency (time and resources used)
